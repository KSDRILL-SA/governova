PREAMBLE / TP00 How to Use This Constitution 1 CRITICAL Rule
TP00 The 80% Rule — Don't Let Process Become Tyranny CRITICAL

This constitution is aspirational, not absolute. Compliance is expected 80% of the time. The remaining 20% is reserved for emergencies, deep flow states, shipping deadlines, or explicit team agreement to temporarily suspend a rule. If you are compliant 100% of the time, you are over-optimizing process and under-optimizing product.

Enforcement
Quarterly review — if compliance exceeds 95%, audit for process burden
Violation Example
Team spends more time on process than building; stress over missing a retro
Compliance Example
"We skipped retro this week to ship auth. We'll do a double-retro next week."
Industry Source
Agile Manifesto ("Individuals and interactions over processes and tools")
📖
Treat this as case law, not a rulebook. You don't read the entire legal library before starting a business. You consult it when you have a specific problem. Don't memorize this document — learn where to look when you need guidance.
⚠️ WARNING SIGNS YOU'RE OVER-APPLYING THIS CONSTITUTION
  • Team groans when "standup time" is mentioned ("Standup Theater")
  • Retro action items are copied/pasted week after week without progress
  • People spend more time updating status than doing work
  • The constitution is cited to win arguments rather than solve problems
  • Compliance is tracked as a metric itself
✅ IF YOU SEE THESE SIGNS: Invoke TP00 and reduce process immediately.
§ Part 1 — Team Roles & Responsibilities (TP1–TP4)
Purpose

Clear ownership, accountability, and decision authority. Everyone knows who does what.

PART 1 / TP1–TP4 Team Roles & Responsibilities 2 High · 2 Medium
TP1Roles DocumentedHigh

Roles MUST be clearly defined for each team member. For solo founders: document your own responsibilities (I am responsible for all technical, product, and operational decisions).

Enforcement
Onboarding + annual review
Violation
Team of 3, no one knows who owns database decisions
Compliance
docs/team/roles.md with clear assignments
TP2Constitution OwnersHigh

Each constitution MUST have a designated owner (person accountable for enforcement, review, and updates). For solo founder: you own all. For team of 2+: assign owners.

Enforcement
Quarterly review + ownership document
Violation
Backend constitution outdated for 6 months, no owner
TP3Decision Authority DocumentedMedium

Decision authority MUST be documented (who decides what: technical, product, operational). One page maximum.

Compliance
"Tech decisions → CTO/Lead. Product decisions → Product Owner. Operations → Founder."
TP4Weekly 1:1 Meetings (Team Only)Medium

Weekly 1:1 meetings between lead and each team member REQUIRED (30 minutes). Not applicable for solo founder.

Enforcement
Calendar audit + team health check
Violation
No 1:1s for 3 months
§ Part 2 — Meeting Cadence & Structure (TP5–TP9)
Meeting Timeboxes
Daily Standup15 min (team) / 5 min (solo)
Sprint Planning45-60 min / 20-30 min
Retrospective30 min / 15 min
Monthly All-Hands30 min (team only)
PART 2 / TP5–TP9 Meeting Cadence & Structure 2 High · 3 Medium
TP5Daily StandupMedium

Daily standup REQUIRED. Team: 15 minutes (what did you do yesterday, what today, blockers). Solo founder: 5 minutes personal (written or recorded).

TP6Sprint PlanningHigh

Weekly sprint planning REQUIRED. Team: 45-60 minutes (prioritize backlog, assign tasks). Solo founder: 20-30 minutes (review priorities, plan week). If backlog is already groomed and clear, 30 minutes is sufficient.

TP7Sprint RetrospectiveHigh

Weekly sprint retrospective REQUIRED. Team: 30 minutes (what went well, what went wrong, what to improve). Solo founder: 15 minutes (personal retro). May be skipped during launch/crunch weeks with explicit team agreement (invoke TP00).

📝
Retro Format: What went well? (5 min) → What went wrong? (10 min) → What will we improve? (10 min) → Action items (5 min)
TP9Meeting DisciplineMedium

Meetings MUST start on time, end on time, have clear agenda shared 24 hours in advance.

TP9aCalendar Bloat AwarenessMedium

Be aware of total meeting load. For a 5-person team: Sprint Planning (60 min) + Retro (30 min) + Dailies (75 min/week) = ~3+ hours/week per person in ceremony. If the team expresses meeting fatigue, reduce timeboxes immediately.

⚠️ Warning Sign: "Standup Theater"
People going through motions, no real value. Cancel meetings that don't have clear agenda/need; use async updates when possible.
§ Part 3 — Communication Guidelines (TP10–TP13)
Communication Channels
  • 📧 Email → Async, non-urgent
  • 💬 Slack/Discord → Day-to-day
  • 📞 Phone → Urgent only
  • 📝 GitHub/Linear → Task discussion
PART 3 / TP10–TP13 Communication Guidelines 1 High · 3 Medium
TP10Async FirstMedium

Async communication preferred (Slack, GitHub, Linear) over sync for non-urgent matters.

TP11Urgent CommunicationMedium

Urgent matters: phone call or immediate Slack @here (not email).

TP12Working HoursHigh

Daily communication window: 9 AM - 5 PM local time. No expectation of response outside these hours. Prevents burnout.

TP13Public Status UpdatesMedium

Status updates MUST be posted in public channels (not DMs) for visibility. For solo founder: maintain public roadmap or changelog.

§ Part 4 — Decision-Making Framework (TP14–TP17)
Decision Types
Reversible (Fast): Deploy feature flag, change copy, update style
Irreversible (24h review): Choose database, change auth provider, remove API
PART 4 / TP14–TP17 Decision-Making Framework 2 High · 2 Medium
TP14Decisions DocumentedHigh

Decisions MUST be documented (ADR for technical, PRD for product). For solo founder: lightweight decision log.

TP15Disagree and Commit (with Safety Valve)Medium

Disagreements follow "disagree and commit" — after decision made, everyone commits (no sabotage, no "I told you so"). PREREQUISITE: Psychological safety must exist for genuine disagreement. If the Health Check (TP24) shows low psychological safety scores, pause and fix team dynamics before enforcing.

🛡️
Safety Valve: If quiet voices are never heard or decisions are dominated by loud voices, pause TP15 enforcement and run an anonymous survey on decision-making comfort first.
TP16Decision Authority by SizeMedium

Small decisions (1 hour or less impact) = individual. Large decisions (significant impact) = team discussion. For solo founder: all decisions are individual.

TP17Reversible vs IrreversibleHigh

Reversible decisions can be made quickly; irreversible decisions require more scrutiny (written proposal, 24-hour review period).

§ Part 5 — Onboarding & Knowledge Sharing (TP18–TP21)
Purpose

Scale team, preserve knowledge, reduce bus factor.

PART 5 / TP18–TP21 Onboarding & Knowledge Sharing 3 High · 1 Medium
TP18Onboarding ChecklistHigh

Onboarding checklist MUST exist for new team members. For solo founder: document for "bus factor" (if you're unavailable, someone can take over).

TP19Documentation as Definition of DoneHigh

Documentation MUST be kept up-to-date — part of definition of done for every feature.

TP20Monthly Knowledge SharingMedium

Knowledge sharing sessions REQUIRED monthly. Team: lunch & learn (30-60 min). Solo founder: self-learning (1 hour — read a paper, watch a talk, document takeaways).

TP21Feature RunbooksHigh

Every major feature MUST have documented runbook before launch.

§ Part 6 — Continuous Improvement (TP22–TP24)
Health Check Questions
1. Clear goals?
2. Resources to do job?
3. Psychologically safe?
4. Feel heard when disagree?
5. Not experiencing burnout?
6. Proud of what building?
7. Meeting load appropriate?
PART 6 / TP22–TP24 Continuous Improvement 1 High · 2 Medium
TP22Retrospective Action ItemsHigh

Retrospective action items MUST have owners and due dates.

TP23One Experiment Per SprintMedium

One improvement experiment per sprint (small change to try). For solo founder: one personal experiment per sprint.

TP24Quarterly Health CheckMedium

Quarterly team health check REQUIRED (anonymous survey: morale, burnout, process satisfaction, psychological safety). For solo founder: monthly 5-minute self-check (journal: energy 1-10, stress 1-10, enjoyment 1-10). This is the canary in the coal mine — low scores require immediate action, not just documentation.
